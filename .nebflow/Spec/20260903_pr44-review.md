## 4. 给外部贡献者 puerhile-maker 的英文回复草稿（作者过目后再发，本节点不发布）

> Hi @puerhile-maker,
>
> Thank you for this thoughtful PR — the problem you identified is real (the 0.42 glass tint can read as disabled at a glance), and the investigation and state-by-state treatment (distinct `:active`, dimmed icons on disabled, keeping the disconnected hover guard) are all well done. Your disabled/disconnected change from grey to the faint-green family actually fits our design language better, and we'd like to keep that part.
>
> Unfortunately we can't merge this as-is, for one blocking reason: **the enabled state abandons the glass material.** Our interactive controls follow a product-wide "light-green glass" language — `rgba(7, 193, 96, 0.42)` + `backdrop-filter: blur(8px)` + inset edges + a whisper border — and `#send-btn` is one of its reference implementations (other controls' docs literally point at it: "Same material as #send-btn / .glass-control"). At 0.92 alpha, and with fully opaque hover/active fills (`#07C160` / `#06A952`), the button reads as a solid chip rather than glass, which breaks that standard across the product, in both themes.
>
> Could we achieve the same affordance goal inside the glass family? For example:
>
> - Enabled: raise the tint within the glass range, e.g. `rgba(7, 193, 96, 0.55–0.65)`, strengthen the border to ~`rgba(7, 193, 96, 0.3)` and boost the green glow slightly — noticeably more tappable, still glass.
> - Hover: deepen within the glass family (e.g. ~0.7) rather than going solid; keep the boosted glow.
> - Active: a further deepened glass step (or a subtle inner shadow) instead of an opaque fill.
> - Disabled / disconnected: your faint-green version (`rgba(7, 193, 96, 0.16)` + dimmed icon) is great as-is — please keep it.
>
> One small extra: the new `#send-btn:disabled svg { opacity: 0.45 }` also applies in the frozen input-bar state (`#input-bar.frozen #send-btn[disabled]`), which previously kept the icon at full strength — a follow-up rule there (or a maintainer ruling) would be welcome.
>
> Thanks again for a clean, well-scoped, single-file change — happy to review a revision quickly.
